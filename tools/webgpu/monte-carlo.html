THE IDEA

More points.
Less uncertainty.

A quarter circle of radius 1 fills π/4 of a unit square. Uniform random points land inside it with that same probability. Count the hits, divide by all samples, and multiply by four.

Sampling error typically shrinks like 1/√N: four times the samples roughly halves the uncertainty. A 95% interval is a method that covers true π in about 95% of repeated experiments; any single run can miss.
