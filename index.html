The goal is not to reduce democracy to a score. The goal is to make the assumptions between “this sounds good” and “this works under real constraints” visible enough to debate, simulate, revise, and reproduce.

Helios · collective intelligence

Turn millions of voices into a plan you can inspect.

Helios is the deliberation layer in front of the simulator: people write what they need, value, fear, and propose; the system organizes those inputs without erasing disagreement, generates candidate plans, and sends them into HappyBearLandia for stress-testing.

The Helios loop

Listen → synthesize → simulate → learn.

The AI is not the sovereign. It is a transparent coordination layer. Citizen writing stays attached to interpretations, dissent remains visible, and public institutions remain responsible for real decisions.

Verification layer

Verify the model—not “the future.”

HBL can make a proposal more trustworthy by exposing what would have to be true for it to work, how sensitive the result is, what evidence supports each link, and where the model fails.

✓

Simulation is not proof that a policy will work.

It is a way to make a proposal falsifiable, inspectable, and comparable. The strongest HBL result is not “the model says yes.” It is: “Here are the assumptions, here are the worlds where it works, here are the worlds where it fails, here is the evidence, and here is what we should test next.”
