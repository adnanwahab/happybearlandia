# Synthetic population behavior model card

This module presents exploratory synthetic model outputs. It does not claim empirical calibration, predictive accuracy, policy causality, or observed human behavior. Map coordinates can supply geography; default incomes, preferences, housing and job capacities remain assumptions.

| Behavior | Actual implementation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Activity and destination | population/behavior.js; clock, employment, seeded schedules and need-driven flexible choices | Simplified district destinations; no observed activity diary |
| Travel mode | Recorded alternatives and mode components in decisionTrace | District travel approximation; inspect-only alternatives are distinguished from recorded decisions |
| Housing search | Household affordability, savings, capacity and utility decisions | District capacities rather than individual leases; displacement risk is not a verified displacement |
| Employment and economy | population/economy.js and behavior.js | Simplified demand and job capacity, not a validated labor market |

The coefficient registry in world/behavior/coefficients/registry.js labels its values analyst-assumption, confidence low. Calibration dataset, date, error metrics and calibrated ranges are null. A registered coefficient is not evidence of its use in a runtime equation. Consult recorded components and active coefficient references. Parameter min/max bounds are engineering limits, not empirically supported domains.

The panel uses authoritative full-model aggregate metrics and cohorts. Visible agents are a bounded render sample. Income cohorts can change membership between snapshots, so differences of cohort means are descriptive rather than matched-person effects. Selected household stories quote actual inspected state and retained decisions; absent detail remains absent. Decision traces and recent event buffers omit older records. The activity ledger separately preserves lifetime aggregates plus bounded hour/day buckets and explicitly tracked routines; it does not invent discarded or untracked personal history.

Sensitivity runs use actual independent runtime futures: at most 1,000 diagnostic people, two paired seeds, one day and baseline plus up to three levers. Results retain their manifests. Sampling changes shared capacity and demand; these runs are diagnostics rather than citywide elasticities or empirical uncertainty bounds. Selected paired actors use nonmutating future inspection and the shared analysis comparator; missing or inconsistent weights make weighted totals unavailable.

Generation target checks use engineering review tolerances and are not independent validation. Observed scorecards compare only matching statistics and units. Missing district outputs are not filled with city totals. Move rates require identical observation periods. Source provenance is supplied by the user and is not independently verified. Calibration candidates and imports require Studio review and never modify the runtime directly.

Population behavior 1.1.0 consumes elapsed time across schedule, arrival and commitment boundaries. Actual fractional travel time is separated from destination activity time; needs use the corresponding interval. Food, fatigue and recreation needs influence seeded flexible choices, subject to simplified time, budget and access constraints. This is a more responsive synthetic model, not validation against observed human diaries.

Population behavior 1.2.0 adds synthetic child ages 0–17, same-household guardians, at most four reciprocal peers, age-gated unescorted travel, and school, recess, sleep, screen, care and seasonal camp routines. Actual committed intervals supply attendance, sleep, screen, fatigue and academic-exposure proxies. These are not grades, clinical outcomes or measured child wellbeing. School-bound bus/rail departure counts describe existing modeled transit trips, not a dedicated school fleet. Supplied school/care/camp locations are used where available; otherwise the service location is an explicit district proxy. Guardian escorts and driving licenses are not modeled.

Childcare seats, affordability and service access constrain enrollment. The designated guardian's committed care-related absence reduces the existing daily wage calculation; planned care availability also gates existing seeded job-search odds. Attended care costs enter the existing household outflow once. Subsidy exposure is separate from family payment and is not a government expenditure ledger. Families and demographics remain synthetic; ages do not advance over these representative runs. Raw and represented child-time totals are distinct, and fatigue/exposure ratios disclose their observed-time denominators. Serialized 1.1.0 population states and Reality futures reject rather than silently replaying under 1.2.0 behavior; frozen housing models are unchanged.

Population behavior 1.3.0 adds `employment.v1`. Civil dates, weekdays, school weekends and summer windows share the immutable `calendarStartEpochDay` initial condition. Work schedules, four-day off-days, assumed notice, after-hours contacts and eligible remote roles affect actual activities and travel. Seeded illness, family and vacation requests exercise bounded leave entitlements; leave changes absence and pay, not disease transmission or clinical recovery. Clinical effects, occupational licensing recognition and portable benefits funds remain unavailable.

The existing household settlement now consumes actual accrued work/leave pay once, alongside the existing nonwage resource assumption. The existing employer cost calculation consumes the matching represented payroll once. Recruitment requires an actual vacancy and sufficient firm budget. Unassigned workers cannot earn wages, and closing an employer removes its jobs rather than silently transferring staff. Productive staffing constrains business revenue; a four-day week can leave staffing gaps even when annual contract pay is retained. Observed job-condition diagnostics can affect seeded turnover, with no direct happiness adjustment. Childcare still constrains actual availability; paid family leave can replace the corresponding lost pay without charging or crediting a second wage ledger.

Employment metrics distinguish raw worker-minutes and household USD from represented employer USD. School-bound transit remains a bus/rail departure proxy, not a dedicated fleet. Advance schedule-publication dates are declared notice assumptions, not invented historical messages. Existing household monthly-cost approximations and exogenous nonwage resources remain simplified; this is not a calibrated payroll, tax, labor-law or benefits-administration model. Earlier population behavior states and futures reject rather than silently replaying under 1.3.0; frozen housing models remain unchanged.

Runtime persistence uses `await runtime.exportCheckpoint()` and `await runtime.restoreCheckpoint(checkpoint)`. Checkpoints are JSON-safe `population-checkpoint.v1` envelopes with model version 1.3.0 and a 32 MiB UTF-8 JSON limit. The typed graph encoding preserves numeric bytes, undefined values, maps, and shared references. Capture drains admitted live-worker tasks and prevents new mutations until the committed model and runtime controls are captured together; it never advances residents to recreate a missing checkpoint. Restore validates before replacing the worker-owned model, restores weather owner state and command cursors, and retains the actual stored history, journals, children, employment queues, economy, selection, view, clock controls, world context, and chart series. Histories already omitted under model retention limits cannot be recovered. Oversized saves and incompatible versions reject explicitly. A restored run cannot be regenerated from seed after worker loss or rewound before its checkpoint; restore its saved checkpoint again instead. Storage, access control, sharing URLs, and page camera/UI persistence are handled by the caller.

Living World explicitly selects `createPopulationRuntime({initialMinute:360,stepMinutes:1,clockMode:'minutes-per-second',...})` for a fresh 6:00 AM population. This creates fresh synthetic home locations, needs and resources at that time; it does not simulate or claim overnight prehistory. The first tick is 360, the history begins at minute 360 with zero observed agent-minutes, and every subsequent committed interval is recorded normally. Other model/runtime callers still default to midnight. Resets retain the chosen fresh origin; checkpoints preserve it and never apply this shortcut to existing runs. Population future manifests include the same initial minute, and replay uses that origin and absolute tick. The fresh origin must lie within the first day and align with the caller's fixed step (futures use 30 minutes).

House mode is an optional additive `house.v1` interior on the same population state, not a separate person or cash simulation. `inspectHousehold(id)` returns actual `memberDetails` and a deterministic `house` description without creating interior state, choosing a resident, charging money or advancing time. A validated `householdAction({householdId,type,personId?,personIds?,roomId?,durationMinutes?,object?})` can admit cooking, shared dinner, rest, reading, music, cleaning, workspace setup and selected furniture upgrades. Catalogs declare prices, 15–120 minute bounds, minimum ages, required objects and capacities. Furniture and additional discretionary ingredients debit existing savings/spending exactly once; objects/servings appear only after actual elapsed installation/preparation. The baseline food allowance remains unchanged. Actual house activity intervals use the existing needs, activity history, journals, supervision, relationships and employment ledgers. Rooms have declared light/noise/privacy/cleanliness factors; these scale actual recovery or eligible remote-work productivity. No happiness or unsupported emotional/clinical need is invented. Unopened homes and old 1.3 saves without optional interiors keep their existing mechanics; unsupported interior versions reject.

`observeHousehold({householdId,minutes:1440,signal,onProgress})` observes the entire population, preserving its fixed step, daily wages/rent/economy and calendar. It executes batches of at most eight unchanged fixed commits and yields between batches; only presentation snapshots are sampled per batch. Progress exposes actual elapsed minutes and committed ticks. Cancellation waits for the current bounded batch and leaves the last committed model intact, without terminating/replaying the worker. The operation is capped at 1,440 minutes and 20 million whole-population agent-steps. House state retains at most 128 changed homes, 32 active residents, 16 objects/home, 32 meal servings/home, 64 completed actions/transactions per home, and 256 runtime action commands. All retained interiors, pending actions, transactions and command cursors are preserved in full checkpoints. Larger checkpoints reject instead of truncating authoritative state.

`walkPerson(personId,{x,z})` or `{locationId}` admits an actual walking trip at the committed clock. It validates the existing walking-distance and minor-permission rules, current travel and guardian constraints, and active house actions. It never teleports or advances a paused clock. The existing straight-line district travel approximation commits positions, need changes, trip completion and history over subsequent model time; no street-level route claim is made. The resident pauses briefly at arrival and resumes its existing schedules. Movement commands and active routes survive checkpoints and deterministic replay.
