# Synthetic population behavior model card

This module presents exploratory synthetic model outputs. It does not claim empirical calibration, predictive accuracy, policy causality, or observed human behavior. Map coordinates can supply geography; default incomes, preferences, housing and job capacities remain assumptions.

| Behavior | Actual implementation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Activity and destination | population/behavior.js; clock, employment, seeded schedules and need-driven flexible choices | Simplified district destinations; no observed activity diary |
| Travel mode | Recorded alternatives and mode components in decisionTrace | District travel approximation; inspect-only alternatives are distinguished from recorded decisions |
| Housing search | Household affordability, savings, capacity and utility decisions | District capacities rather than individual leases; displacement risk is not a verified displacement |
| Employment and economy | population/economy.js and behavior.js | Simplified demand and job capacity, not a validated labor market |

The coefficient registry in world/behavior/coefficients/registry.js labels its values analyst-assumption, confidence low. Calibration dataset, date, error metrics and calibrated ranges are null. A registered coefficient is not evidence of its use in a runtime equation. Consult recorded components and active coefficient references. Parameter min/max bounds are engineering limits, not empirically supported domains.

The panel uses authoritative full-model aggregate metrics and cohorts. Visible agents are a bounded render sample. Income cohorts can change membership between snapshots, so differences of cohort means are descriptive rather than matched-person effects. Selected household stories quote actual inspected state and retained decisions; absent detail remains absent. Decision traces and recent event buffers omit older records. The activity ledger separately preserves lifetime aggregates plus bounded hour/day buckets and explicitly tracked routines; it does not invent discarded or untracked personal history.

Sensitivity runs use actual independent runtime futures: at most 1,000 diagnostic people, two paired seeds, one day and baseline plus up to three levers. Results retain their manifests. Sampling changes shared capacity and demand; these runs are diagnostics rather than citywide elasticities or empirical uncertainty bounds. Selected paired actors use nonmutating future inspection and the shared analysis comparator; missing or inconsistent weights make weighted totals unavailable.

Generation target checks use engineering review tolerances and are not independent validation. Observed scorecards compare only matching statistics and units. Missing district outputs are not filled with city totals. Move rates require identical observation periods. Source provenance is supplied by the user and is not independently verified. Calibration candidates and imports require Studio review and never modify the runtime directly.

Population behavior 1.1.0 consumes elapsed time across schedule, arrival and commitment boundaries. Actual fractional travel time is separated from destination activity time; needs use the corresponding interval. Food, fatigue and recreation needs influence seeded flexible choices, subject to simplified time, budget and access constraints. This is a more responsive synthetic model, not validation against observed human diaries.

Population behavior 1.2.0 adds synthetic child ages 0–17, same-household guardians, at most four reciprocal peers, age-gated unescorted travel, and school, recess, sleep, screen, care and seasonal camp routines. Actual committed intervals supply attendance, sleep, screen, fatigue and academic-exposure proxies. These are not grades, clinical outcomes or measured child wellbeing. School-bound bus/rail departure counts describe existing modeled transit trips, not a dedicated school fleet. Supplied school/care/camp locations are used where available; otherwise the service location is an explicit district proxy. Guardian escorts and driving licenses are not modeled.

Childcare seats, affordability and service access constrain enrollment. The designated guardian's committed care-related absence reduces the existing daily wage calculation; planned care availability also gates existing seeded job-search odds. Attended care costs enter the existing household outflow once. Subsidy exposure is separate from family payment and is not a government expenditure ledger. Families and demographics remain synthetic; ages do not advance over these representative runs. Raw and represented child-time totals are distinct, and fatigue/exposure ratios disclose their observed-time denominators. Serialized 1.1.0 population states and Reality futures reject rather than silently replaying under 1.2.0 behavior; frozen housing models are unchanged.

Population behavior 1.3.0 adds `employment.v1`. Civil dates, weekdays, school weekends and summer windows share the immutable `calendarStartEpochDay` initial condition. Work schedules, four-day off-days, assumed notice, after-hours contacts and eligible remote roles affect actual activities and travel. Seeded illness, family and vacation requests exercise bounded leave entitlements; leave changes absence and pay, not disease transmission or clinical recovery. Clinical effects, occupational licensing recognition and portable benefits funds remain unavailable.

The existing household settlement now consumes actual accrued work/leave pay once, alongside the existing nonwage resource assumption. The existing employer cost calculation consumes the matching represented payroll once. Recruitment requires an actual vacancy and sufficient firm budget. Unassigned workers cannot earn wages, and closing an employer removes its jobs rather than silently transferring staff. Productive staffing constrains business revenue; a four-day week can leave staffing gaps even when annual contract pay is retained. Observed job-condition diagnostics can affect seeded turnover, with no direct happiness adjustment. Childcare still constrains actual availability; paid family leave can replace the corresponding lost pay without charging or crediting a second wage ledger.

Employment metrics distinguish raw worker-minutes and household USD from represented employer USD. School-bound transit remains a bus/rail departure proxy, not a dedicated fleet. Advance schedule-publication dates are declared notice assumptions, not invented historical messages. Existing household monthly-cost approximations and exogenous nonwage resources remain simplified; this is not a calibrated payroll, tax, labor-law or benefits-administration model. Earlier population behavior states and futures reject rather than silently replaying under 1.3.0; frozen housing models remain unchanged.

Runtime persistence uses `await runtime.exportCheckpoint()` and `await runtime.restoreCheckpoint(checkpoint)`. Checkpoints are JSON-safe `population-checkpoint.v1` envelopes with model version 1.3.0 and a 64 MiB UTF-8 JSON limit. The typed graph encoding preserves numeric bytes, undefined values, maps, and shared references. Capture drains admitted live-worker tasks and prevents new mutations until the committed model and runtime controls are captured together; it never advances residents to recreate a missing checkpoint. Restore validates before replacing the worker-owned model, restores weather owner state and command cursors, and retains the actual stored history, journals, children, employment queues, economy, selection, view, clock controls, world context, and chart series. Histories already omitted under model retention limits cannot be recovered. Oversized saves and incompatible versions reject explicitly. A restored run cannot be regenerated from seed after worker loss or rewound before its checkpoint; restore its saved checkpoint again instead. Storage, access control, sharing URLs, and page camera/UI persistence are handled by the caller.

Living World explicitly selects `createPopulationRuntime({initialMinute:360,stepMinutes:1,clockMode:'minutes-per-second',...})` for a fresh 6:00 AM population. This creates fresh synthetic home locations, needs and resources at that time; it does not simulate or claim overnight prehistory. The first tick is 360, the history begins at minute 360 with zero observed agent-minutes, and every subsequent committed interval is recorded normally. Other model/runtime callers still default to midnight. Resets retain the chosen fresh origin; checkpoints preserve it and never apply this shortcut to existing runs. Population future manifests include the same initial minute, and replay uses that origin and absolute tick. The fresh origin must lie within the first day and align with the caller's fixed step (futures use 30 minutes).

House mode is an optional additive `house.v1` interior on the same population state, not a separate person or cash simulation. `inspectHousehold(id)` returns actual `memberDetails` and a deterministic `house` description without creating interior state, choosing a resident, charging money or advancing time. A validated `householdAction({householdId,type,personId?,personIds?,roomId?,durationMinutes?,object?})` can admit cooking, shared dinner, rest, reading, music, cleaning, workspace setup and selected furniture upgrades. Catalogs declare prices, 15–120 minute bounds, minimum ages, required objects and capacities. Furniture and additional discretionary ingredients debit existing savings/spending exactly once; objects/servings appear only after actual elapsed installation/preparation. The baseline food allowance remains unchanged. Actual house activity intervals use the existing needs, activity history, journals, supervision, relationships and employment ledgers. Rooms have declared light/noise/privacy/cleanliness factors; these scale actual recovery or eligible remote-work productivity. No happiness or unsupported emotional/clinical need is invented. Unopened homes and old 1.3 saves without optional interiors keep their existing mechanics; unsupported interior versions reject.

`observeHousehold({householdId,minutes:1440,signal,onProgress})` observes the entire population, preserving its fixed step, daily wages/rent/economy and calendar. It executes batches of at most eight unchanged fixed commits and yields between batches; only presentation snapshots are sampled per batch. Progress exposes actual elapsed minutes and committed ticks. Cancellation waits for the current bounded batch and leaves the last committed model intact, without terminating/replaying the worker. The operation is capped at 1,440 minutes and 20 million whole-population agent-steps. House state retains at most 128 changed homes, 32 active residents, 16 objects/home, 32 meal servings/home, 64 completed actions/transactions per home, and 256 runtime action commands. All retained interiors, pending actions, transactions and command cursors are preserved in full checkpoints. Larger checkpoints reject instead of truncating authoritative state.

`walkPerson(personId,{x,z})` or `{locationId}` admits an actual walking trip at the committed clock. It validates the existing walking-distance and minor-permission rules, current travel and guardian constraints, and active house actions. It never teleports or advances a paused clock. The existing straight-line district travel approximation commits positions, need changes, trip completion and history over subsequent model time; no street-level route claim is made. The resident pauses briefly at arrival and resumes its existing schedules. Movement commands and active routes survive checkpoints and deterministic replay.

Dining chairs also support a single-resident `householdAction({householdId,type:'sit',personId,roomId:'kitchen',seatIndex?,durationMinutes:30})`. Sitting needs no meal or cash, uses the existing 15–120 minute duration bounds, and records actual HOME seated minutes. Energy changes by the declared chair recovery rate (`-.035 + .06 * roomRecovery` per committed minute); fullness, fun and social continue their background decay. Exact seat indices are zero-based and bounded by the actual dining-table capacity. Sitting and shared meals reserve the same seat inventory; new reservations retain their exact slots, while older saved meal actions derive deterministic slots observationally. Inspection exposes `interaction.objectId`, `interaction.seatIndex`, and actual action progress. Food rules for shared dinner are unchanged. Existing interiors without seated-minute counters or seat arrays restore without eager mutation; new counters appear only when new seated activity is committed.

Explicit replacement uses `householdAction({...request,personId,interrupt:true})`; the selected actual person ID is required. Availability uses an observational admission projection that removes only that resident's existing locks and returns `willInterrupt[]`. Every age, home, object, seat, food, other-participant and cash check must pass before any interruption, event or charge is committed. A successful replacement returns `interrupted[]`, preserves elapsed need/time effects, retains paid costs, and grants no unfinished servings, object or refund. Leaving a shared meal removes only the selected actor; remaining participants keep their progress and exact seats, and already plated food is not returned. Interruption records are bounded to 64 per home. A not-yet-departed home-origin walk can be replaced without moving coordinates; a resident already away cannot teleport home for an interior action. Requests without the explicit flag retain the previous busy rejection behavior. Full checkpoints and retained command replay preserve replacements deterministically.

Household workflow extension: resident task queues retain stable IDs, one running task and up to 15 pending tasks per resident (32 sparse queues maximum). Admission while paused does not execute, advance time, reserve food, or spend savings. Fixed-step boundaries start feasible tasks, record blocking reasons, and reconcile each completion once. Paid cancellation retains consumed ingredients and costs; a direct replacement preserves pending tasks. Departing visitors retain their population/household IDs and cancel only the host home's remaining tasks.

Hygiene, available bladder capacity, and comfort are illustrative satisfied-need meters from 0 to 100. Fresh and pre-extension saves initialize them to 75, 75, and 65 respectively, without replaying history. Background decay is .025, .04, and .015 points per simulated minute. Shower/toilet/sleep/eat use actual fixtures, attendance, room capacity, and elapsed rates. Bathrooms supplied by migration are labeled synthetic baseline affordances; they are not surveyed indoor mapping or clinical health measures.

House build edits consume existing household savings and use separately bound frozen saved-site geometry. Commands cannot supply their own geometry or balances. Reserved furniture cannot be sold, and active household activities block structural edits and undo/redo. Conversation completion updates the existing social pair once, preserving deterministic preferences and memories. Romantic intent requires explicit consent by the exact adult, nonfamily pair and never derives from friendly conversation alone.

### Neighborhood hotspots

The world toolbar and map markers expose up to 24 gathering places. Supplied parks, plazas, cafés and food destinations are preferred. If none exist, up to four existing building access points are explicitly labeled proposed plazas; a map without building locations has no invented hotspots. Places open from 07:00–22:00 in model time. Recreation and dining destination choices use distance, seeded variation and place appeal, with existing work/school, affordability, travel-time and weather constraints retained. This is an illustrative behavioral assumption, not measured foot traffic.

Benches (+0.35 appeal), food stalls (+0.45) and greenery (+0.30) are free city-design interventions. They change subsequent destination choices without resetting residents, advancing time or charging a household. Their small scene props reflect committed installations. Save/restore retains improvements and command replay reapplies them at their original model minute.

Visitor counts include only people actually present in dining or recreation, excluding travelers. Every 30 modeled minutes, at most four pairs per hotspot can have a real proximity-checked conversation through the existing relationship system. Friendship may improve or a misunderstanding may occur; outcomes are seeded. The last 120 neighborhood encounters are retained, with five shown per place. Simulation steps create encounters; rendering and inspection do not.

### Weekly neighborhood traditions

The Neighborhood traditions panel offers a Saturday market (09:00–13:00), Friday park music (18:00–21:00), and Saturday outdoor movie (19:00–21:00), aligned with the configured model calendar. Each uses an actual hotspot, can be paused, and can move venues between gatherings. Stalls, extra seating and a small stage are free design support, adding 0.15 outing appeal each. Event equipment appears in the world while the event is active, with a screen for movie night.

Stable seeded preferences encourage familiar attendees to return each week. Recorded friends may invite one another during the hour before an event, bounded to 16 invitations per occurrence. Invitations increase outing appeal but cannot override mandatory schedules, weather, energy, affordability or travel constraints. Only actual co-presence changes an invitation to attended. Unfulfilled invitations become missed, rather than recording an invented meeting.

Attendance requires a resident to be stationary in dining/recreation at the venue at both ends of a committed interval. This conservative measure does not credit arrival travel as event attendance. Counts are unique residents, not represented population estimates. The last twelve gatherings and 192 invitations persist in the model checkpoint; the panel shows recent attendance, person-minutes and returning regulars. Support, venue and enabled changes use the authoritative worker command timeline for replay and save/restore.


### Household financial pressure

The `Households at risk` lens colors the bounded visible people sample by the actual household payment history: red for exhausted savings or no more than seven days of net depletion runway, amber for less than 30 days of expense coverage or no more than 30 days of net depletion runway, green above those thresholds, and gray for insufficient history. Selected residents retain their selection highlight. These are descriptive display rules for synthetic households, not calibrated predictions.

The worker retains at most 14 compact daily settlement summaries per household, including their opening/closing balances and actual wages, expenses, rent and childcare charges. Moving payments update the same summary. Seven consecutive, reconciled, full-day intervals are required for estimates; partial startup days, unexplained balance changes, missing intervals and stale histories prevent extrapolation. Legacy records without a recorded period start cannot establish a complete interval. Expense coverage is savings divided by average daily spending assuming no income. Net depletion runway is savings divided by average daily spending minus income, and is absent when cash flow is not negative. Two full weeks are required for a trend; daily net changes within $1 count as steady. Wage declines and rent/childcare increases compare those same weeks. Household taxes remain unmodeled.

Live diagnostics use worker-recorded payments without additional simulation steps. The economic records viewer recomputes risk from collected SQLite settlements for only the sampled households in the selected snapshot, with a cutoff at that snapshot's simulation minute. It never uses later payments or treats unsaved live summaries as collected evidence. The supporting Follow the money view honors the same cutoff. Sample counts are not citywide population estimates. Histories, summaries and selection survive normal full checkpoints.


### Household intervention calculator

Inspecting a household in Households at risk exposes a What would help this household calculator in both live and collected views. Its baseline is the same latest seven complete, reconciled payment days used by financial pressure. The risk summary carries the actual average daily wages, rent and household-paid childcare, together with the evidence window and snapshot time. Short, stale, partial or incomplete histories do not produce estimates.

Rent reduction, childcare subsidy and wage increase controls each accept 0–100%; the projection horizon is 1–365 whole days. The comparison shows each intervention alone and their combination. Rent relief reduces the recorded rent outflow, childcare support reimburses only recorded household-paid care, and wage support scales actual received wages. A household without wages receives no invented job or wage income. Public childcare cost, added employer payroll and landlords' rent forgone are reported separately, per synthetic household, over the chosen horizon. Their sum equals the additional household resources. Full childcare/rent relief cannot exceed recorded charges.

Savings projections use current savings plus daily net cash flow multiplied by the horizon. Work hours, employment, benefits, other spending, prices and behavior stay constant; tax, administration, business viability and general market responses are outside the calculation. Negative savings remain visible as an uncovered deficit. The calculator executes no policy, changes no population state and writes no simulation records. Its JSON export includes settings, baseline, scenarios, evidence bounds, source run/snapshot context and assumptions. The supporting payment link retains the selected snapshot cutoff.

## Playable Life matched follow-up

New resident reports fork the exact paused checkpoint before player commands. The autonomous control receives no player commands and advances through the same 24-hour day plus the selected 30- or 90-day follow-up at the checkpoint’s fixed step. The existing `playable-life.v1` report retains its trajectory fields and adds `arm` and `matchedControl`: seed, modelVersion (also rules version), starting runId, entityId, startTick and endTick. The control also records `after` and an empty `actions` list. Starting checkpoint configuration and world context are the scenario inputs; accepted commands and their minutes remain in the report. Needs use model points and savings use USD. Both arms use the same resident identity and clock without a cross-model mapping. The horizon locks at handoff and remains identical across both arms and pause/resume.

The panel subtracts control outcomes from played outcomes only when provenance matches; unavailable measurements stay unavailable. Reports without compatible arms are observational. This synthetic comparison describes the complete player command sequence in one seeded run, with no sampling uncertainty estimate or empirical causal claim. The control runs on demand, resumes after cancellation, and is destroyed when the report is saved or the page closes.

## Research-only transport ablation

Research-only transport ablation hooks (`transport-experiment.js`) are inert
unless explicitly attached to a fresh model by the headless research runner.
The full arm preserves ordinary state exactly. Frozen, shuffled, half and double
transport feedback leave household, care, housing, firm and social dependencies
active. They do not constitute resident independence. Controls and streamed
observers are not production checkpoint state; regenerate from the versioned
recipe. [E2 protocol and limitations](../../docs/research/million-agent-architectures.md)
defines the output graph and the withheld release claim.
