# SHE-229 r2 — Scientific skeptic: persuasive but misleading futures

Review date: 2026-11-12. Analysis only; no product or model changes.

The simulator can help investigate the consequences of its programmed assumptions. It cannot, on reproducibility or visual richness alone, establish what would happen in a real city or why. A causal explanation of code execution is useful evidence about the model; external causal identification requires additional evidence. The most dangerous output is a precise, reproducible answer to an inadequately specified question.

## Twenty ways conclusions can mislead

### 1. A recorded dependency becomes a real-world causal claim

**Scenario:** A housing WHY trace links faster permits to lower modeled rent. A viewer reads this as evidence that permitting reform caused a real city's rent decline, although demand also changed.

**Falsifiable test:** In a fixed checkpoint/seed comparison, change only permitting; separately run a no-op and a demand-only intervention. Reject the claimed model attribution if the no-op changes rent, pre-intervention outcomes differ, or the cited path is absent from recorded inputs/events. Passing establishes conditional model causality only.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Put paired deltas beside the dependency trace; distinguish programmed edges, observed associations and unidentified real-world effects using text and line styles.

**Engineering improvement:** Extend existing causal notices with claim type, intervention identity, competing mechanisms and an explicit external-identification status. Never translate a trace into a causal percentage.

### 2. A policy bundle hides confounding among its components

**Scenario:** Plan A changes permits, vouchers, density and funding. Its benefit is credited entirely to density because density is the headline.

**Falsifiable test:** Run a matched-seed 2×2 density-by-voucher factorial with other parameters identical. Calculate the interaction `Y11 − Y10 − Y01 + Y00`; reject an additive explanation if its magnitude exceeds a predeclared substantive tolerance.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Show four outcome distributions and the interaction's paired-seed distribution, including negative and zero effects.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse explicit parameter diffs and the housing reversion/residual contract. Support factorial comparison manifests; keep nonadditive reversions labeled as conditional contributions, not shares of total causation.

### 3. Behavioral assumptions predetermine the policy winner

**Scenario:** The aggregate rent-vacancy response guarantees a convincing supply story, while landlord pricing, household sorting or credit constraints are inadequately represented. Housing uses an illustrative rent-response coefficient, not estimated local behavior.

**Falsifiable test:** Freeze the plan and seed set; sweep registered `rent_response`, adoption and resistance across declared plausible bounds. Reject the claim of robust ranking if any admissible combination reverses it. An empty empirical basis for the bounds is itself reported.

**Uncertainty visualization:** A ranking map over assumptions with an uncalibrated-region hatch and missing-evidence label.

**Engineering improvement:** Require assumption-range provenance and expose ranking reversals. Alternative behavioral equations require separately versioned models and validation, not silent coefficient changes.

### 4. Synthetic residents acquire the authority of real geography

**Scenario:** Recognizable OpenStreetMap streets make generated incomes and modeled district assignments seem like observed neighborhood conditions.

**Falsifiable test:** Trace every displayed metric to its source field and dataset; reject any observed-data claim whose lineage ends at a synthetic generator. For a proposed real-data adapter, compare joint age/income/tenure distributions against an independently sourced target, including unsupported groups.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Separate geometry provenance from social-data provenance; show target-versus-generated distributions and coverage, with unavailable targets left blank.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse SHE-96 metric evidence at the point of interpretation. Require geographic boundary and population-support metadata before calling an output a local estimate.

### 5. A lucky seed becomes a policy success story

**Scenario:** One compelling saved future is selected after examining many seeds, then presented as typical.

**Falsifiable test:** Before inspecting outcomes, fix a seed list, metric, horizon and practical benefit threshold. Run every pair; reject “consistently beneficial” if any retained valid pair worsens that metric, and report the improvement fraction with its denominator. Do not hide ties or failed pairs.

**Uncertainty visualization:** All paired deltas on a shared axis, zero and practical-threshold lines, plus a marker for the showcased future.

**Engineering improvement:** Attach selection method and ensemble rank to shared examples. Preserve the full attempted seed set and separate a curated story from an ensemble summary.

### 6. Monte Carlo precision is mistaken for total uncertainty

**Scenario:** Increasing runs to 1,000 produces a stable mean; users infer that an uncertain behavioral model is now reliable.

**Falsifiable test:** Repeat across independent seed batches with fixed assumptions, then across a predeclared parameter grid. Reject the claim that more seeds resolve uncertainty if scenario rankings still vary across admissible assumptions after seed estimates stabilize.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Separate within-assumption seed spread, between-assumption spread and unquantified structural uncertainty. Label p10–p90 as empirical model quantiles, never a confidence interval for reality.

**Engineering improvement:** Keep sample count separate from epistemic-evidence status. Reuse the paired-comparison range notice; label which uncertain inputs were sampled and which remained fixed.

### 7. Independent input draws understate correlated crises

**Scenario:** Financing stress, construction cost and adoption are sampled independently, making simultaneous adverse conditions too rare. Decision parameter streams are independently derived by parameter ID.

**Falsifiable test:** Compare the existing sampler against a declared joint adverse scenario with identical marginal ranges. Reject independence-based robustness if the policy fails a predeclared cost or supply threshold under the joint scenario while passing independently sampled runs. This tests dependence sensitivity, not real crisis frequency.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Input-pair scatterplots and a tail-loss curve for each dependence assumption.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse composable stress presets immediately; a future versioned joint-distribution contract should record dependencies and their evidence. Do not assign empirical probabilities to authored stresses.

### 8. Omitted feedback makes short-run benefits look durable

**Scenario:** Lower rents attract migration or shift investment outside the boundary; a model with insufficient endogenous response overstates lasting affordability.

**Falsifiable test:** Compare an explicit feedback-enabled model variant against a frozen-input variant using matched external shocks. Reject “feedback is negligible” if long-horizon rank or practical-threshold status changes. First audit whether each proposed loop already exists; current housing has demand, vacancy and financing responses but that does not prove all loops are covered.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Time-series differences between structural variants, with modeled and omitted loops labeled in a diagram.

**Engineering improvement:** Maintain a feedback inventory beside the ODD/contracts. Expose supported exogenous migration sensitivity now; adding endogenous relocation needs a versioned mechanism and independent validation.

### 9. A fitted history masquerades as a forecast test

**Scenario:** A low historical error is used as proof of policy accuracy after fitting to the same data, or after overlapping survey estimates leak information across periods.

**Falsifiable test:** Freeze release-vintage cutoff, training data, coefficient grid and holdout before fitting. Compare held-out error with a simple persistence baseline; reject predictive superiority if it does not improve on that baseline under a predeclared loss. The Houston overlapping-vintage diagnostic explicitly does not qualify as a strict forward backtest.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Training/holdout boundary, observation release dates, observed errors and baseline errors; show unavailable survey covariance rather than inventing intervals.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse cutoff-aware imports and calibration. Require a distinct evaluation identity, holdout dataset and baseline before displaying a validated-forecast claim.

### 10. Simpson's paradox reverses the apparent distributional result

**Scenario:** Mean rent falls citywide as the mix of rental units shifts, although rent rises within every original income or district group.

**Falsifiable test:** On a model with supported group identities, compare pooled change with within-group change under frozen baseline weights. Flag a reversal when pooled and all supported within-group effects have opposite signs. Aggregate housing alone cannot supply resident-level strata.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Paired group slopes, baseline weights and standardized versus pooled estimates, with group sample coverage.

**Engineering improvement:** Add composition-aware summaries only where identities and denominators exist. Keep aggregate-model subgroup effects unavailable; never manufacture households from a city mean.

### 11. Neighborhood averages are attributed to individuals

**Scenario:** A district income gain is narrated as a named resident's improved finances, even though that person lost work or belongs to another simulation authority.

**Falsifiable test:** Follow a claimed individual's checkpoint ID and clock to their own recorded outcome. Reject the personal claim if no verified mapping exists or their record contradicts it; compare supported individual changes with the aggregate sign.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Individual trajectory over the district distribution, with “identity mapping unavailable” replacing the personal overlay when unsupported.

**Engineering improvement:** Gate person-specific narrative templates on recorded entity/time mappings. Reuse the product's separate-authority boundary; location or similar names are insufficient identity evidence.

### 12. Goodhart's law rewards a proxy at the expense of welfare

**Scenario:** A plan scores well on housing supply or a homelessness proxy while increasing fiscal burden or harming residents omitted from the score. Housing's displacement, employment and homelessness outputs are labeled proxies.

**Falsifiable test:** Freeze the optimization metric, then evaluate the chosen plan on predeclared withheld outcomes and budget constraints. Reject “best overall” if a withheld constraint fails. Unsupported real-welfare outcomes remain unknown, even when every modeled metric passes.

**Uncertainty visualization:** A Pareto frontier with uncertainty per supported outcome, explicit proxy labels and a column for unmodeled harms.

**Engineering improvement:** Keep outcome components and constraints visible; preserve evaluation criteria before search. Prevent a composite score from upgrading proxies to observed welfare.

### 13. Policy comparisons use unequal resources or starting conditions

**Scenario:** A heavily funded plan beats an unfunded baseline and is called more efficient, despite different initial states, horizon, or real-dollar basis.

**Falsifiable test:** Audit manifests for identical start, rules, units, horizon and matched shocks; then compare equal-budget variants using actual delivered expenditure. Reject efficiency claims without comparable cost denominators. Near-zero incremental cost must not produce an infinite efficiency score.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Paired cost-effectiveness scatter with budget lines and an explicit incompatibility panel.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse compatibility checks and housing expenditure ledgers; add resource-normalization requirements to decision briefs. Report effectiveness and cost separately when normalization is unsupported.

### 14. Missing outcomes disappear from the denominator

**Scenario:** Hard scenarios fail or are cancelled; averages over successful runs make a fragile plan look safer. Decision aggregation exposes failed counts, but metric summaries use finite successful outcomes.

**Falsifiable test:** In an integration fixture, induce failures in adverse pairs and compare complete-case ranking with declared best/worst feasible bounds for missing outcomes. Reject an unconditional ranking when bounds permit reversal; assert attempted, completed, paired and metric-evaluable counts reconcile.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Outcome distribution plus a visible missing/failed strip and bounded sensitivity interval; distinguish missing from genuine zero.

**Engineering improvement:** Extend existing failed/paired counts to metric-level denominators and reasons. Do not silently impute zero or drop the unavailable arm when reporting risk.

### 15. A chosen endpoint hides delay, reversal or damage

**Scenario:** Year ten looks favorable while years one through five carry unaffordable burdens; another chosen horizon reverses the ranking.

**Falsifiable test:** Predeclare decision horizons and evaluate every supported recorded period. Reject “benefits throughout” on any adverse period; compare cumulative expenditure and transition harms separately from terminal stock. Annual housing records cannot establish unrecorded daily effects.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Paired delta trajectories with zero line, intervention date, unavailable periods and duration below a practical threshold.

**Engineering improvement:** Require horizon in every summary/link, reuse stored monthly/yearly trajectories, and disclose cumulative versus endpoint semantics. Never interpolate an unobserved event as recorded evidence.

### 16. Implementation feasibility is reduced to optimistic sliders

**Scenario:** A costly construction promise appears effective under full adoption but fails when resistance, delays and constrained capacity are applied together.

**Falsifiable test:** Fix policy text and budget; compare default implementation with documented low-adoption, delay and capacity stress combinations. Reject feasibility robustness if delivered homes or actual expenditure fails a predeclared target in any declared admissible case. Passing authored cases does not estimate implementation probability.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Planned versus delivered spending and completions across cases; highlight undelivered appropriations and pipeline delays.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse existing delivery factors, capacity ledger and stresses. Require explicit feasibility assumptions in briefs; keep announced funding separate from actual modeled spending.

### 17. Searching many alternatives produces a spurious winner

**Scenario:** After dozens of tweaks, a discovery engine highlights the one plan that wins on a favorable finite seed set.

**Falsifiable test:** Lock the selected plan after exploration, then evaluate on a previously unseen seed batch and predeclared primary metric. Reject the original generalization if the benefit misses its predeclared practical threshold on confirmation; retain all explored plans and failed confirmation attempts.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Exploration-versus-confirmation performance and rank distributions across batches, showing the entire candidate count.

**Engineering improvement:** Separate exploration and confirmation records in the experiment registry. Freeze objective, inputs and seed allocation before confirmation; disclose adaptive search history.

### 18. A unit or denominator mismatch creates an apparent effect

**Scenario:** All-home vacancy is compared with rental vacancy, nominal dollars with base-year dollars, or a Population minute with a City month. The housing contract already warns about these denominator differences.

**Falsifiable test:** Inject mismatched units, geography, observation period and denominator into a comparison fixture. Reject the display as comparable unless an explicit tested conversion exists; verify known hand-calculated conversions and boundary dates.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Side-by-side metric definition cards with denominators and time bases; incompatible values remain separated, with no connecting effect arrow.

**Engineering improvement:** Extend existing unit/model compatibility with denominator and dollar-year semantics. Reuse independent state authorities and fail closed on unsupported time/entity adapters.

### 19. Rich animation and selective narrative imply evidence that is absent

**Scenario:** A realistic city and a dramatic bakery closure make an illustrative model seem locally observed; a headline's selected future is mistaken for an expected outcome.

**Falsifiable test:** Audit every numeric and causal statement against its exact stored record, including run, entity and time. Reject unsupported statements. In a proposed randomized comprehension study, compare identical results with/without dramatic rendering; test a preregistered increase in false “observed/predictive” classifications. That user study has not been run.

**Uncertainty visualization:** Story marker on the full ensemble with percentile, selection rule and persistent synthetic/observed labels; include contradictory outcomes.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse record-linked headlines and narrative provenance. Gate templates by evidence availability, show counterexamples, and keep interpretation visibly separate from stored events.

### 20. Deterministic replay and scale benchmarks substitute for validity

**Scenario:** A repeatable million-object scene or fast pair-transfer fixture is marketed as one million socially interacting residents whose forecasts are trustworthy.

**Falsifiable test:** Validate the receipt's represented, explicit, active, interacting, cohort and drawn counts against its pinned adapter; reject scale claims beyond measured support. Separately compare an external held-out behavioral outcome with a declared baseline. Exact replay passes only the reproducibility claim, never that external validity test.

**Uncertainty visualization:** An evidence matrix separating replay, performance, interaction semantics, calibration and external validation; use measured/unavailable states rather than a single confidence badge.

**Engineering improvement:** Reuse SHE-153's receipt validator and claim limits. Pin model/rules/backend/device and benchmark workload. Keep scientific-validity acceptance separate from rendering and throughput acceptance.

## Five experiments users can run through existing product controls

These are proposed protocols, not executed user studies or newly implemented features. Source-backed controls exist; browser interaction was not rerun in this documentation task. If the route or controls are unavailable in the running build, report that blocker rather than replace the protocol with invented results. Use small batches first and stay within normal UI limits. Every result remains conditional on a synthetic model.

Common record: download the manifest and export the editable project from Decision Studio; retain model/rules versions, compiled parameter maps, uncertainty distributions, stresses, master seed and derived run seeds, horizon, run IDs, attempted/completed/evaluable counts, units and selected metric. Freeze the hypothesis and practical threshold before running; preserve unsuccessful runs. A changed configuration is a new experiment. Empirical seed ranges describe these runs, not calibrated probabilities about real cities.

### Experiment A — Reproduction as a negative control

**Question/hypothesis:** Does an unchanged saved experiment reproduce its numerical outcomes?

**Entry and procedure:** Open `/world/decision.html`, choose **Try housing example**, review compiled assumptions, disable automatic preview, set 10 futures, 10 years, seed 42, and **Simulate this plan**. Download the manifest, then use **Reproduce experiment** without editing inputs. Record both result identities and compare all supported numerical metrics for the same scenario/seed; metadata such as capture time may differ.

**Falsifier and reading:** Any numerical mismatch under the same pinned rules/inputs falsifies exact replay. Missing or incompatible versions are explicit reproduction failures, not close-enough success. Passing says nothing about real-world accuracy.

**Inspection:** Compare distributions and the same selected future before/after; retain a metric-by-metric equality table outside the UI if necessary. Do not claim the UI has an automated whole-run equality view. The API create/execute/manifest/reproduce journey is covered by the integration suite.

### Experiment B — Permit-only intervention and causal specificity

**Question/hypothesis:** Under this model, faster permitting lowers year-ten rent relative to current policy without assuming additional funding.

**Entry and procedure:** In Decision Studio use the reviewed example's current-policy scenario; duplicate it and change only the registered permitting target (for example, 18 to 6 months if both remain valid). Keep all other parameter maps, uncertainty and stress presets equal. Set 100 futures, 10 years, seed 42. Run and inspect matched comparisons, rent, housing supply and actual expenditure. Download the configuration so “only one change” can be checked.

**Falsifier and reading:** Predeclare a positive practical rent reduction in base-year dollars; reject the practical-benefit hypothesis if the mean paired reduction falls short. Any pre-intervention difference undermines the control. A benefit here tests programmed housing behavior, not an identified permitting effect in reality.

**Inspection:** Use existing future distributions and selected-run records; report improvement fraction, pair count and spending alongside the rent result. A dedicated full paired-delta plot is a proposed improvement, not assumed present.

### Experiment C — Implementation stress challenge

**Question/hypothesis:** The selected plan's rent benefit survives low adoption and implementation delay within a fixed public budget.

**Entry and procedure:** Duplicate a reviewed plan twice: unstressed, low-adoption, and low-adoption plus implementation-delay cases. Use the existing stress-preset controls, preserve the same baseline, 100 futures, seed 42 and ten-year horizon. Record exact preset IDs and effective assumptions; inspect supply, rent and public expenditure.

**Falsifier and reading:** Reject robustness if any declared case misses the predeclared rent-benefit threshold or violates the budget constraint. Distinguish unspent appropriations from cheap successful delivery. No frequency can be assigned to an authored stress case without evidence.

**Inspection:** Compare case distributions and the spending-versus-supply outcomes. Preserve all cases in the exported project instead of sharing only the favorable one.

### Experiment D — Seed-batch confirmation and sample-size sensitivity

**Question/hypothesis:** A policy ranking chosen in a small exploratory sample survives more runs and a new seed batch.

**Entry and procedure:** In Decision Studio freeze the baseline/alternative parameters and primary metric. Run 10 futures with master seed 42, then 100 and, if practical, 500 with the same seed. These nested samples are not independent confirmations. Freeze the selected policy before a separate 100-future run at master seed 4242; retain the actual derived seed sets and check overlap before calling it independent.

**Falsifier and reading:** Reject ranking stability if the mean paired delta changes sign across the preregistered batches or the confirmation misses the practical benefit threshold. More runs cannot validate omitted mechanisms or unjustified input distributions.

**Inspection:** Compare empirical spreads, paired counts and improvement fractions across saved experiments; use a small manual table if the UI cannot overlay batches. Do not describe overlapping batches as replicated evidence.

### Experiment E — Commute benefit versus budget tradeoff

**Question/hypothesis:** A larger supported transit investment improves the selected resident's modeled commute sufficiently to justify its modeled cost.

**Entry and procedure:** Open `/world/living-city/`, choose the commute request, and select one supported investment option. Use **Compare 100 paired futures**; record the option, cost, starting seed and horizon. Inspect a selected pair, its baseline/intervention timeline, event record and shared challenge recipe. Repeat with a second supported investment, holding the starting scenario fixed. This page uses 100 samples; do not claim an editable sample count or arbitrary intervention support.

**Falsifier and reading:** Predeclare a commute reduction target and maximum acceptable cost; reject the investment hypothesis if either condition fails. If the two runs do not share verified initial state/paired seeds, withhold the dose comparison. A one-pair council decision is illustrative and cannot replace the ensemble.

**Inspection:** Read the existing matched-commute summary, pair chooser, same-seed scorecard and cost record. Report worse/no-change pairs as well as improvements. Do not infer a citywide travel effect or resident consent from this selected participant.
