## Evidence and interaction contract

Every screen keeps four named lanes visible, including in text-only mode:

* **Observed model events (O):** records actually emitted or retained by the selected
  simulation, with event ID, run/arm, entity, month and payload. They are observations
  of a synthetic execution, never observations of real residents. Field snapshots
  are labeled snapshots when no event was recorded.
* **Causal evidence (E):** either a cited recorded model input/transition dependency,
  or a controlled comparison changing one declared decision under fixed starting
  state, rules and matched seed. Identify which kind supports each claim. A path
  explains model operation; a controlled comparison estimates an effect within
  that model. Neither establishes real-world causation or a unique mediator.
* **Correlations (C):** co-occurrence, association or parallel change. Show dotted
  unarrowed connectors, excluded from mechanism traversal and causal rankings.
* **Speculation (S):** optional authored or generated interpretations, visibly
  separated from records. No fabricated quotations, feelings or probabilities.
  Default story voice: “In this modeled branch …”; hypothetical voice is labeled.

Missing lanes say “No evidence recorded” or “Not evaluated,” never silently
collapse. Solid arrows require resolvable same-run evidence; assumed mechanisms
carry their rule/version and synthetic-assumption label. Changed values without
an edge remain valid differences with unknown attribution. A blank descendant
list means no retained link, not no downstream effect.

A proposed universal entry action, **Explore another path**, opens on a selected
person, building, business or neighborhood and preserves run/entity/time. It first
checks model support, retained start state and a valid intervention. Unsupported
selections can inspect recorded history and explain the missing capability;
they cannot launch an invented future. City-only supported identities form the
first slice. Map positions require source geometry or an explicitly schematic
layout; no generated parcel coordinate is presented as a recorded location.

Every comparison pins initial-state digest, model/rules/generator versions,
source attribution, intervention target/value/units/timing, seed, horizon,
arm IDs and entity mapping. Branches preserve the parent. One-seed differences
have **no uncertainty interval**. Ensembles declare a preselected seed list,
paired valid/missing counts, summary definition and sampled spread; seed spread
is distinct from structural and empirical uncertainty. Assumption sweeps change
one assumption per matched comparison, not secretly in the intervention arm.
No global happiness score substitutes for explicit outcome dimensions.

## Fifteen proposed interactions

### 1. The Empty Footprint — building

Click a building and hold **Without this decision**. Its outline becomes an
empty schematic footprint while a synchronized two-arm timeline shows when the
inventories first diverge. Release or use a keyboard toggle to return. A caption
asks, “What exists here in one modeled future?” rather than inventing an occupant.

**O:** cited completion event plus building/unit snapshots in each arm. **E:**
common-start one-policy comparison supports the inventory difference; only a
recorded completion mapping supports the project-to-building link. **C:** nearby
rent changes appear in a separate strip without arrows. **S:** “a gathering place
that never opened” is an optional imagined vignette, not an engine result.

Show missing geography as a labeled schematic and distinguish absent building
from unavailable snapshot. Readiness: strongest near-term candidate; current
Future Machine supports inventory comparisons, not geographic placement or a
complete causal graph. A single seed conveys one modeled path.

### 2. The Hinge Drawer — any changed entity

Drag the outcome card backward through its evidence graph. Each recorded input
opens a small drawer: **Test this decision instead**. The selected metric and
horizon stay fixed. A ranked shelf asks which tested intervention changed that
metric most, leaving untested candidates visibly unranked.

**O:** actual events and decision records. **E:** rank only effects of separately
executed, supported, one-decision ablations from the same start; graph degree or
edge weight is not importance. **C:** simultaneously occurring milestones stay
in a parallel timeline. **S:** a narrator's “turning point” is an interpretation
unless backed by the declared test/ranking.

Ties, signed effects, missing pairs and interactions remain visible. “Most” means
among tested interventions for this outcome, never the city's single true cause.
Requires an allowlisted ablation interface; existing WHY navigation alone does
not implement this experiment.

### 3. Two Doors, One Household — person

Click a supported City resident and open two doors onto the same household in
control and intervention arms. A shared month dial changes housing assignment,
rent burden and employment cards together. A small household ledger puts costs
beside gains; names are explicitly synthetic.

**O:** retained assignments, moves and household budgets, with verified resident-to-
household mapping. **E:** the paired intervention estimates this household's modeled
outcome difference; a move explanation requires the relevant recorded mechanism.
**C:** neighbors moving in the same month do not explain this move. **S:** imagined
relief or regret belongs in an opt-in story card, never a purported resident quote.

A departed or missing resident remains a missing outcome, not a replacement person.
Current City identities are usable in principle; a House/Population handoff is
unsupported without a tested adapter. Emotional focus comes from continuity and
visible tradeoffs, not claims of measured wellbeing.

### 4. The Benefit Ledger — neighborhood

Click a neighborhood and unfold a street into two aligned lists: households and
firms gaining or losing on selected measures. A map links each row to verified
locations where available. Switch independently between rent burden, housing
access, wages and public cost; never sum incompatible units into a moral verdict.

**O:** same-start cohort memberships and recorded arm outcomes. **E:** paired
within-model differences identify effects on declared measures; “benefited” means
improvement under an explicit user-selected direction. **C:** district averages
and demographic associations are contextual. **S:** fairness judgments and
unmeasured harms are identified as values or hypotheses.

Keep entrants, departures and unavailable records in separate counted rows;
report the starting-cohort denominator to avoid survivor bias. Public spending
is a shared ledger, not invented personal tax incidence. Requires supported
household/firm metrics and mappings; aggregate housing supply alone cannot establish
who benefited. Nonmonetary harm remains unmeasured.

### 5. The Borrowed Tomorrow — business

Click a business and send a pulse through its recorded dependencies. A split
cashflow calendar asks, “Did this decision buy time, or shift a cost?” Expanding
a delayed consequence reveals the original input and later outcome together.

**O:** retained operating periods, delivery availability, budgets and closures.
**E:** only recorded supplier/transport links animate; a supported link-closure
comparison measures business effects under its stated economic-domain assumptions.
**C:** neighboring closures and market-wide trends remain side annotations.
**S:** owners' motives or a neighborhood's lost identity are authored possibilities.

Do not bridge the economic fork's business periods to monthly City without an
explicit clock adapter. Show the interval-delivery assumption beside each result.
A horizon ending before failure is censored, not proof of survival. Requires
configured fork authentication and retained compatible economic state.

### 6. The Ripple with a Missing Stone — policy on an entity

Click a decision in an entity's history. Trace outgoing links across a graph
beside a location panel and chronological event cards. At an unrecorded dependency,
the animated ripple stops at a torn edge labeled **We cannot trace farther**.

**O:** cited event IDs at each reached node. **E:** same-run recorded mechanisms
with source evidence; the path is an explanation of execution, not an indirect-
effect estimate. **C:** associations remain dotted and do not propagate pulses.
**S:** possible unmodeled consequences sit beyond the tear, never fill it.

Honor the current Ripple limit of five edges total and disclose truncation,
cycles, missing citations and unfinished runs. Schematic layout is sufficient.
Near-term reuse of the inspector is possible; estimating mediation would require
separate intervention experiments. The emotional beat is seeing both the reach
of a choice and the honest boundary of knowledge.

### 7. The First Open Window — neighborhood or building

Drag a decision token along a monthly timeline. Each evaluated start month opens
or closes a window onto a declared endpoint, such as a completed home by month H.
The user sees the first tested feasible opportunity and the last tested useful one.

**O:** recorded policy stages, capacity and completion times for each evaluated run.
**E:** each timing test differs from its matched control only by the declared
schedule; enumerate every allowed month before claiming an earliest feasible point.
**C:** a capacity peak near success is not automatically the reason. **S:** “the
moment the city could have saved itself” is an optional interpretation.

No binary search assumes monotonicity: quarterly cadence and resource competition
can create disconnected windows. Missing checkpoints or unsupported schedules
mark unknown windows. State earliest **within the tested legal domain**, not all
history. Requires timed-intervention support and full reruns; ordinary checkpoint
scrubbing does not answer this question.

### 8. The Assumption Weather — any outcome

Open an outcome and slide one documented assumption, such as utility capacity,
through a declared range. Small multiple maps or schematic inventories show
where the conclusion persists, reverses or becomes unavailable. A narrative
caption changes only when the measured direction changes.

**O:** stored outcomes and execution failures for every tested setting. **E:**
within each setting, compare policy and control under identical rules and matched
seed; sensitivity across settings tests robustness, not empirical truth. **C:**
cross-setting covariation is not an identified real-world relationship. **S:**
untested ranges and alternative mechanisms stay explicitly hypothetical.

Show exact range, units and version; unsupported inputs require a new versioned
recipe, not an arbitrary live edit. Failed runs are not zero effect. No shading
means probability unless a sampling design justifies it. The current flagship's
synthetic utility assumption makes this useful, but a supported sweep orchestration
and persistence contract are prerequisites.

### 9. The Same City, Different Dice — neighborhood

Fan a neighborhood into paired postcards, one per preselected seed. Selecting a
postcard opens that exact pair, not a newly sampled city. A dot plot shows the
signed policy difference and lets the visitor find an ordinary or unusual path.

**O:** retained outcomes and events for each seed/arm. **E:** each pair changes one
decision from its common start; aggregate paired differences describe sensitivity
to the declared seed sample. **C:** seed-specific patterns like high employment
alongside lower rent are associations unless separately tested. **S:** a postcard's
imagined daily life is labeled narrative.

Show sample size, missing pairs, observed range and any declared interval method;
never “chance this will happen in your city.” Entity IDs need only match within
a pair: no claim that resident 7 is the same person across generated seeds.
Requires bounded ensemble execution and saved pair selection; current one-seed
replay alone supplies no distribution.

### 10. The Unwritten Address — building or person

Click a newly added home and open an address book with two columns: actual modeled
assignments and blank spaces the model cannot fill. A person row can open its
supported household history; an empty unit remains empty instead of acquiring an
invented family. The emotional center is possibility with a visible boundary.

**O:** unit inventory, occupancy and actual assignment events. **E:** the policy
pair supports added supply; connecting a specific household to an assignment
requires retained rules/events and mapping. **C:** new units and district population
growth may coincide. **S:** imagined future tenants are explicitly authored,
never evidence that someone was housed.

Differentiate vacant, unavailable, unobserved and not-yet-built. Entity creation
in one arm has no forced twin in the other. Can begin with current housing
inventory; richer assignment stories need verified evidence beyond completion.

### 11. The Detour Atlas — business or neighborhood

Select a link closure and compare route ribbons before and after it. Traveling
along a supported route opens cost and access cards for affected entities. An
unrouted trip is rendered as an unresolved destination, not a zero-minute journey.

**O:** recorded closures, routes, travel metrics and endpoints. **E:** a supported
single-link intervention and actual routing dependencies support model effects;
a route picture alone is insufficient. **C:** shop revenue changes nearby are
associations without a linked business mechanism. **S:** “customers abandoned this
street” requires evidence or remains a hypothesis.

Label City district routing as a single-pass proxy; do not show invented street-
level navigation. Separate the economic logistics fork from City transit rules.
Requires a supported model-specific link action and retained route data. Missing
geography gets a schematic network.

### 12. The Echo Calendar — person or neighborhood

Fold a timeline so that a recorded event and its later descendants align in
stacked calendar pages. The visitor follows one supported household through
immediate effects, delayed effects and unresolved intervals.

**O:** time-stamped events and retained later snapshots. **E:** only time-respecting
recorded dependencies connect pages; paired endpoint changes do not identify
the intervening mediator by themselves. **C:** repeating monthly patterns sit in
a separate cadence lane. **S:** “the family never recovered” is speculation unless
a defined metric and adequate follow-up support a narrower statement.

Mark lag units, retention gaps, horizon and model cadence. No ancestor arrow can
travel backward in time; feedback appears at later time-indexed nodes. Requires
retained longitudinal evidence, not a fabricated continuous biography. No daily
Population or generational extension is implied.

### 13. The Unsent Letter — person or business

Open two brief letters from alternative futures. Each factual sentence has an
expandable record citation and an evidence-lane badge. The reader can switch to
an unadorned event table; unsupported sentiments appear only in a separately
headed imagined paragraph.

**O:** verified assignments, budgets or closures transformed into bounded factual
sentences. **E:** sentences saying “because” require a cited mechanism or a precisely
qualified controlled-comparison statement. **C:** “that month also…” explicitly
signals association. **S:** first-person thoughts and emotions are labeled fictional
voice and never attributed as actual model testimony.

Missing evidence suppresses the factual sentence. A source change invalidates
the letter; citations keep run/entity/month fixed. Proposed composition on top
of existing records, not a new resident language or emotion model. Reviewable
text templates should precede generative narration.

### 14. The Fork That Rejoins — building or neighborhood

Scrub two timelines that first separate, then appear to converge. A zipper joins
only the selected metric lane; other lanes stay apart. Click the seam to see
whether the entire supported state or merely the headline value matches.

**O:** recorded metric trajectories, inventories and available full-state digests.
**E:** the one-decision comparison supports a transient measured effect; full-state
identity requires a complete canonical comparison, not equal rounded rent. **C:**
two metrics recovering together remains co-movement. **S:** “the city forgot the
policy” is an interpretation, not evidence of erased history.

Retain cumulative cost and differing event histories after endpoint convergence.
No universal rejoining claim follows from missing data. Reuses paired histories;
a metric-specific comparison must declare precision, tolerance and units. Particularly
useful as a zero-effect or short-lived-effect counterweight to dramatic stories.

### 15. The Receipt at the Crossroads — any supported entity

Pin one conclusion to a small branching diagram: start → decision → selected
outcome. Turn the card over to inspect/copy the existing receipt, exact selected
month and evidence references. Reopening shows the same supported entity where
mapping exists and explicitly rejects a different run masquerading as the original.

**O:** receipt observations and cited event payloads. **E:** verified recomputation
checks the saved scenario/digests; causal claims still require the corresponding
comparison or dependency evidence. **C:** annotations about coincident events stay
outside the causal diagram. **S:** the user's interpretation travels as an
explicitly separate note, never inside authoritative outcome records.

Use the existing canonical receipt unchanged. Entity focus needs an explicit
navigation mapping outside that schema; do not claim the receipt already encodes
universal entity selection. Unsaved, corrupt, unsupported-version and unavailable-
server states remain visible. A reproducible scenario is not a validated prediction.

## Three demo selections and functional acceptance specifications

Selection criteria are qualitative design judgments: a complete user journey,
reuse of actual interfaces, emotional clarity and honest evidence boundaries.
No user preference, usability score or performance measurement was collected.

| Rank | Selection | Why this experience | Scope boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | #1 The Empty Footprint | A visible single-decision difference and a precise completion event; strongest existing paired-data foundation | Monthly City housing only; schematic placement unless geometry is verified |
| 2 | #6 The Ripple with a Missing Stone | Makes indirect change inspectable and missing causality tangible; reuses recorded Ripple | Existing traced City scenario only; no arbitrary interventions or inferred edges |
| 3 | #15 The Receipt at the Crossroads | Lets another person inspect and reopen the actual result, making trust an action | Existing saved Future Machine pair; no universal fork or checkpoint-verification promise |

These are three experiences, not a forced integration of incompatible runs.
#1 and #15 can share one saved Future Machine pair. #6 uses its own explicitly
identified causal scenario until that same-run trace integration exists. A
future implementer must not silently connect #1's building to #6's fixture graph.

### Demo A — Empty Footprint

Entry: existing homepage Play Demo → `/world/future-machine.html?demo=1` →
select the actual added building in the paired scene. Proposed action: toggle
Without/With while holding month and entity focus; jump to the completion event;
inspect baseline/plan units and cumulative public capital cost.

Prerequisites: existing immutable pair, stable per-frame identity, completion
matcher, supported scene mapping and an accessible text comparison. Use the
recorded current recipe, not hand-authored endpoint values. Test seed 42,
30 households, 120 months, housing d1/24 homes/illustrative USD 1,000,000,
delay 0/compliance 1, current model and manifest.

Acceptance: common Month 0; the current regression's first physical difference
is month 16, building `building-project-policy-1-0`; completion source `16-0`
(with run/arm/month, since the short ID alone is not globally unique); month-16
homes 36/60/+24 and cumulative capital USD 0/1,000,000/+1,000,000. These are
fresh test observations of synthetic rules, not real forecasts. Source records
must drive displayed values. There is no parcel ID for this building; say so.
Null intervention must produce identical histories and no phantom footprint.

Failure acceptance: missing frame/event or unsupported entity shows unavailable
attribution; no arbitrary event fallback. Keyboard toggle, text values and reduced
motion retain all evidence. Proposed browser test must enter from the homepage,
select the actual entity, toggle both ways and confirm stable run/month/units.
This browser journey was not executed for this design-only ticket.

### Demo B — Ripple with a Missing Stone

Entry: `/world/causal.html` → explicitly record/load the fixed deterministic
scenario → choose a real input event → existing inspector Ripple. Proposed
presentation places event cards beside the bounded graph and makes each gap
selectable. No new trace producer is required for this initial experience.

Acceptance: all animated edges resolve to cited same-run events; correlates
never animates; traversal retains seed/model/entity/tick and existing limits.
Clicking a node opens its exact event. Missing evidence, cycles, truncation and
unfinished runs have separate text states. A leaf says no outgoing link retained.
Use reduced motion and text-only list paths. The ordinary five-edge cap remains
visible; do not imply that five edges exhaust the city's dependencies.

Failure acceptance: remove a cited fixture record and require an explicit missing-
evidence gap; a correlation-only fixture must yield no mechanism pulse. Test
foreign run IDs and absent events without substituting another scenario. Proposed
browser verification follows the normal page/inspector route; this ticket verifies
underlying read contracts, not the proposed layout or its emotional effect.

### Demo C — Receipt at the Crossroads

Entry: the saved pair from Demo A → select month 16 → existing Replay receipt →
inspect/copy → reopen on the same disposable service. Proposed crossroads card
links the receipt to that result and separately preserves valid entity focus.

Acceptance: model/manifest/seed/recipe, arm identities, selected month, units,
source events and selected/terminal digests remain inspectable. Reopened history
and regenerated receipt must match. Explicitly label manifest recomputation and
synthetic assumptions. The current receipt does not independently verify every
intermediate checkpoint. Entity navigation uses verified mapping, with an explicit
missing-selection notice if unavailable.

Failure acceptance: corrupt checksum, changed saved outcome, wrong version,
unsaved identity, unavailable saved run and clipboard denial all preserve truthful
states; clipboard denial offers manual copy. No fallback generates a fresh pair.
Proposed normal-entry browser journey covers inspect/copy/paste/reopen and one
corrupt receipt. Current persisted library roundtrip passed; browser clipboard and
CLI network binding remain unverified in this session.
